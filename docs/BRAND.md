# TalentFlow brand direction

This is a working identity for the proof of concept, not a claim of trademark clearance or a finished corporate identity.

## Positioning

TalentFlow helps HR and production managers have better-informed conversations about skills and development. The product should feel clear, credible and human. Its visual language must make assessments understandable without implying that a score determines a person's future.

## Expression

- **Name:** TalentFlow, written as one word, capital T and F in prose.
- **Wordmark:** lowercase `talentflow` in the interface, paired with a compact `t.` symbol. The symbol is built with text and CSS and does not require an external image.
- **Voice:** direct, respectful, supportive and specific. Describe observations and development actions; avoid labels that imply a final hiring or promotion decision.
- **Typography:** system sans-serif, prioritizing legibility and reliable offline operation. Headings use a stronger weight and restrained spacing; body text uses normal sentence case. Informational text is at least 10 px (most at 11 px or more). Contrast has not been measured formally; see the validation note below.

## Color roles

| Role | Light | Dark | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Page background | `#F4F5F7` | `#11141B` | Quiet canvas |
| Primary text | `#191F2B` | `#E9ECF1` | Main content |
| Primary action | `#1E3768` | `#8CA4D2` | Primary buttons; dark text on the light dark-mode button |
| Current assessment | `#214181` | `#839FD4` | Current radar series and values |
| Initial assessment | `#B66C3E` | `#F1BD87` | Initial radar series and values |
| Brand accent | `#315BD4` | `#AEBFFF` | Wordmark and symbol |

Blue provides a stable visual anchor; warm amber separates the comparison series and draws restrained attention to development. Color never carries the meaning alone: series have names and numeric values, and the matrix has text and axis labels. The interface provides light and dark appearances and honors reduced-motion preferences.

## Applying the identity

Use neutral surfaces for dense information, blue for interactive or selected elements, and amber sparingly for supporting emphasis. Avoid using a bright accent as a blanket background. Prefer a clear heading and readable data over decoration. Employee-supplied content remains in its original language; interface copy is available in Italian and English.

Before production use, validate the identity with representative HR and production users, review color contrast and accessibility across target devices, and check the name and mark with the relevant company stakeholders.
